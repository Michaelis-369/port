The Why

Building toward Kigani Systems

Jean Michel grew up in Rwanda watching smallholder farmers lose crops to flooding, counterfeit pesticides, and unaffordable input waste. That's not background context for this portfolio — it's the reason most of it exists. The flood early warning system and the off-grid aeroponic farming system weren't built as class projects to showcase engineering skill in isolation. They were built as the first two proof points of a longer thesis: that AI and low-cost robotics, applied deliberately, can make food production more resilient for the farmers who can least afford to lose a season.

That thesis has a name now — Kigani Systems (working name) — a company Jean Michel is building toward that applies AI and low-cost robotics to agriculture for smallholder farmers. Everything on this site, from embedded controller work to production AI infrastructure, is either a direct step toward that goal or the skill-building required to get there.

Flood early warning (Rwanda) → Off-grid aeroponic farming → AI + automation experience (Plurilock, Ocean) → Kigani Systems
