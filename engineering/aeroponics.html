A soil-free, low-water, low-pesticide growing system designed for the resource-constrained conditions Jean Michel grew up watching smallholder farmers face in Rwanda — flooding, counterfeit pesticides, unaffordable input waste.

Fluid distribution failures since the gravity redesign
The Constraint

No grid. A hard power budget. Eighteen plants that can't wait.

The system had to run entirely off-grid — solar and battery only — while continuously supporting an 18-plant aeroponic array. Every subsystem, especially fluid delivery, had to fit inside a power budget tight enough that a single oversized pump could sink the whole design.

The Decision

Ten nozzle configurations failed. So the architecture changed, not the nozzle.

Pressurized atomizing nozzles are the standard aeroponic delivery method — so that's where the design started. Ten different nozzle configurations were tested against the available pump pressure. All ten failed to atomize reliably under the power budget. Rather than keep tuning a fundamentally mismatched architecture, the decision was to abandon pressurized delivery entirely and redesign around gravity: a perforated acrylic distribution plate that needs no pump pressure at all.

The Build

15+ custom parametric components, fabricated and wired by hand.

  • 15+ parametric CAD components in Fusion 360 & Siemens NX — tilted elliptical press-fit plant holders, zero adhesive, field-repairable
  • Fabricated from HDPE, acrylic, and PVC using a band saw, drill press, and 3D printer
  • Arduino controller, iterated from relay-driven to transistor-based motor drive after diagnosing switching noise on the bench
  • IP65-rated enclosure with full solar-battery power management
  • Complete electrical design: panel selection, charge controller configuration, battery sizing, power budget allocation, wiring harness

Exploded view — plant holder, distribution plate, and manifold repeat across the 18-plant chassis

The Result

18 plants, continuous off-grid operation, three documented design cycles.

The system has run 18 plants continuously off-grid with zero system failures since deployment. The full development history — including the nozzle-to-gravity pivot — is documented across three design cycles in an 8-page technical report.

Power path: panel → charge controller → battery bank → load, sized to the 18-plant budget

Design iteration

v1

Pressurized nozzle delivery. 10 configurations tested and failed under available pump pressure.

v2

Gravity-fed perforated plate replaces nozzles. Relay-driven motor control; switching noise diagnosed on bench.

v3

Transistor-based motor drive, IP65 enclosure, full solar-battery power budget. Zero failures since.
