The Constraint

Manual angle-of-attack adjustment was slow, and it was a safety problem.

Setting angle of attack on the wind tunnel rig required manual adjustment that took hours and posed a hazard every time it happened — a bottleneck for both repeatability and lab safety.

The Decision

Replace manual adjustment with a mechanism, not a procedure.

Rather than write a safer manual procedure, the decision was to design and fabricate a wall-mounted unistrut mechanism with precision index stops — removing the manual hazard by removing the manual step.

2 hrs After

The Build

Full instrumentation stack, plus a calibration pipeline adopted lab-wide.

  • PV panels installed on custom mounting frames; all electrical connections and grounding wired by hand
  • Charge controllers configured; pyranometer and viscometer commissioned
  • Wind tunnel instrumented with force sensors, pressure taps, and velocity probes, with EMI-conscious signal routing
  • Python pipelines (Pandas / NumPy / Matplotlib) to clean and calibrate a 12-month solar irradiance dataset — bias detection and drift correction, formally adopted into lab protocol

Bias detection and drift correction applied to raw sensor readings across 12 months

The Result

Adopted into standard lab procedure.

The unistrut mechanism cut setup time 71% (7 hours to 2 hours), eliminated a hazardous manual adjustment, and was adopted into standard lab procedure. The calibration pipeline's bias-correction methodology was likewise formally adopted into lab protocol.
