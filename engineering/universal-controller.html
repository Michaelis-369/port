Universal Controller

What began as a gaming controller is evolving into a general-purpose, configurable multi-device controller — control anything controllable, reconfigured without ever touching firmware.

Bare-metal STM32 · HAL, no Arduino abstraction 9 PCB iterations
9
Hardware & enclosure iterations
The Constraint

Low-latency analog input, on bare metal, with hardware sourced under real supply constraints.

Reliable joystick-to-host communication demanded ADC conversion and signal processing tight enough to avoid perceptible latency — with no Arduino abstraction layer to hide timing behavior, and components that had to be sourced, verified for compatibility, and procured against real lead times.

The Decision

A factory defect traced without a board re-spin.

A batch of boards came back from fabrication behaving incorrectly. Rather than assume a design flaw and trigger a costly re-spin, the fault was traced on the bench to a single wrong resistor populated on the board by the factory — a component swap fixed every unit, no new PCB revision required.

Schematic-style block layout — defective resistor diagnosed and swapped without re-spinning the board

The Build

Full-stack, from bare-metal firmware to a configurator app for non-technical users.

  • STM32 (Nucleo64 + Bluepill), HAL-level development, ADC conversion for joystick input, signal processing to cut latency
  • Full KiCad PCB schematic and layout across 9 hardware iterations
  • Snap-fit, minimal-fastener enclosure design in Fusion 360 — constraint-driven geometry interfacing electronics to housing, now at v9
  • HID / USART / USB protocol-level debugging for reliable low-latency host communication
  • Component sourcing, compatibility verification, procurement logistics
  • Full-stack configurator app — Python/Flask backend, HTML/CSS/JS frontend, local storage + JSON profile save/load for reconfiguration without reflashing firmware

The Result

Reconfigurable by anyone, without a flash cable.

The controller can be reprofiled for a new device entirely through the configurator app — no reflashing required — making the hardware genuinely general-purpose rather than locked to its original use case. Research is ongoing under Prof. Allan Moser.
