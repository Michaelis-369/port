Apple Hardware Challenge

AC Power Adapter Redesign

A first-principles power path analysis of a laptop AC adapter, with a full loss budget and three GaN-based redesign topologies evaluated on quantitative tradeoffs.

3
GaN redesign topologies evaluated on efficiency, EMI, thermal, and cost
The Constraint

A conventional adapter topology has a loss budget baked into every stage.

Rectification, an LLC resonant converter, synchronous rectification, and output regulation each carry their own conduction, switching, core, and thermal losses. Any redesign proposal had to be justified against a full accounting of where that budget currently goes, not a single headline efficiency number.

The Decision

Quantify the loss budget stage by stage before proposing GaN.

Instead of jumping straight to a wide-bandgap device swap, the analysis mapped losses onto every stage of the power path first — establishing exactly which stages GaN would help, and by how much, before evaluating topology tradeoffs.

Loss budget annotated at each stage of the conventional power path

The Build

Three GaN topologies, compared on more than efficiency.

Each candidate topology was evaluated on efficiency, EMI behavior, thermal performance, and cost — with a full verification and test plan proposed for validating the winning design.

The Result

A quantitative case for a GaN redesign, ready for prototyping.

The analysis produced a stage-by-stage loss budget, a ranked comparison of three viable GaN topologies, and a complete verification/test plan — the deliverable expected for the Apple Hardware Challenge.
